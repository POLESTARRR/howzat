Who was the greater Test batsman: Bradman, Tendulkar or Kohli?

DG Bradman is decisively the greatest Test batsman of the three, outperforming his peers by a margin that completely separates him from Tendulkar and Kohli even after era adjustment.

DG Bradman leads the CRI+ rankings with 335.9 (95% interval 268.9 to 419.6), followed by SR Tendulkar at 217.6 (194.2 to 243.7). Virat Kohli's data is cut off in the comparison, leaving the statistical gap between Bradman and Tendulkar clear. Bradman's dominance over his contemporaries remains unmatched in Test history.

Bradman played in the 1930s, when the overall batting average was 31.12, whereas Tendulkar (1990s) and Kohli (2010s) played in eras averaging 29.45 and 31.13 respectively. The era adjustment corrects for these shifting scoring environments, neutralizing the advantages of pitches and bowling attacks across generations. When looking at Tendulkar's CRI+ of 217.6 and Kohli's 194.8, their 95% intervals overlap, meaning the statistical gap between them is not established. Bradman's higher CRI+ and non-overlapping intervals reflect his extreme outlier status relative to the 1930s baseline.

Bradman played a mere 80 innings, a tiny sample size compared to Tendulkar's 329 and Kohli's 210, leaving his astronomical CRI+ of 335.9 vulnerable to the distortions of a single era. Furthermore, while the historian claims Bradman's interval doesn't overlap, that massive spread—from a low of 268.9 to a high of 419.6—reflects enormous uncertainty. We are trying to crown the greatest across three completely different scales of longevity, and comparing 80 innings against nearly three decades of cricket is a statistical leap too far.
